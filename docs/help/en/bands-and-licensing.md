**TL;DR:** Listening is allowed almost everywhere. Transmitting is not: on amateur (ham) bands you need an amateur license, and most other frequencies belong to services this radio isn't approved for. You're responsible for following your country's rules.

This page is general guidance, not legal advice.

### Listening
In most countries you may listen to amateur, airband, marine, weather and broadcast frequencies. A few countries restrict listening to some services, or what you may do with what you hear.

### Transmitting on amateur bands
- You need an amateur radio license (in the US, at least a Technician license from the FCC).
- Stay inside your license's privileges and your country's band plan. On this radio, 2 m and 70 cm are the usual bands: 144–148 MHz and 420–450 MHz in the US, 144–146 MHz and 430–440 MHz in Europe.
- Identify with your call sign as your rules require.

### Other frequencies
- **Airband, marine, public safety, business**: don't transmit. These need licenses and certified radios.
- **FRS, GMRS, PMR446 and similar "walkie-talkie" services**: they normally require radios certified for that service. This radio generally isn't, even where the frequencies look license-free.
- The **TX lock** column and the radio's **F Lock** band plan help keep you on allowed frequencies. Leave them on unless you know exactly why not.

### Getting licensed
Local clubs run classes and exams, and many welcome visitors.
